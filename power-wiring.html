5 V DC · two 64×32 panels · one supply

Scoreboard Power Wiring

How to feed two chained LED panels and the Pi from a single sealed supply, with every connection you touch sitting at 5 volts.

Power distribution

One supply, two buses, three loads. The panels and the Bonnet each take their own pair straight off the bus rather than borrowing from a neighbour, so no connector carries more current than the load behind it.

Data, which is a separate path entirely

The ribbons between the panels carry signal and nothing else. That is why panel two cannot be fed from panel one: there is no current path between them to borrow.

Three things not to do

  • Do not use an open-frame supply.

    The screw terminals on one of those carry live mains, uncovered. Fine in a grounded enclosure on a bench, wrong for something that sits in a living room and worse for something you hand to somebody else.

  • Do not run panel two's power through panel one.

    The ribbon between them is data. Panel two's current has no business flowing through panel one's power traces, which are sized for one panel.

  • Do not feed both panels through the Bonnet.

    Same reason. Its traces and terminal block were designed around a single panel, and 8 A through them buys you heat and voltage drop.

Before you trust it

  1. With the brick plugged in and nothing else connected, meter across the two lever nuts. You want 5.0 to 5.2 V.

  2. Connect the panels and the Bonnet, power up, and put a full-white frame on the display.

  3. Meter again at the far panel's header while that white frame is up. Still above 4.8 V means the wiring is carrying it. Below that, the gauge or a connection is the problem, not the supply.

  4. Leave it running for an hour, then feel the fuse holder and both panel connectors. Warm is fine. Hot is a connection that is not tight.

Companion to the build runbook. The panel current figures assume 64×32 P4 panels rated near 4 A each at full white; the average draw for a scoreboard is far lower, but the supply has to survive the peak.
